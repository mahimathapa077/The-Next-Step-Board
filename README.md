# The Next-Step Board

An interactive productivity tool designed to help overwhelmed students figure out **what to do next** instead of staring at a giant to-do list.

> I came up with the concept, UX structure, task-sorting system, break logic, and product behavior. I used AI-assisted development tools to turn the idea into a working web app.

## 🚀 The Product

Traditional to-do lists tell you what you have to do, but they don't always help you decide **what deserves your attention right now**.

The Next-Step Board takes a messy task dump and asks a few quick questions about each task before routing it into one of six categories:

- **Do**: Important and urgent
- **Decide**: Important, but not urgent
- **Dash**: Not important, but needs to get done
- **Delegate**: Someone else can handle it
- **Defer**: Save it for later
- **Dump**: Not worth your time

There's also a dedicated **Breaks** section that helps you think about breaks intentionally rather than automatically reaching for your phone.

## 🧠 How It Works

### Task Routing

Each task is evaluated based on:

- Importance
- Urgency
- Consequences of skipping it
- How much you want to do it
- Whether someone else can do it

The answers determine which of the six action categories the task belongs to.

### Break Routing

Breaks are evaluated based on:

- Energy required
- How much they refill you
- How long they take

They are then categorized as:

- **Recharge**: Low-effort breaks that genuinely refill your energy, like resting, taking a short walk, or listening to music.
- **Reward**: Breaks that take a little more energy but feel enjoyable and satisfying, best saved for after important tasks.
- **Reset**: Light activities that help you mentally restart, like stretching, tidying your space, or stepping away for a moment.
- **Drain**: Activities that feel like a break in the moment but often leave you feeling more tired or distracted afterward.

The goal isn't to eliminate breaks. It's to make them more intentional.

## 💡 Why I Built It

I wanted a student productivity tool that didn't just give me **another list of things to do**. I wanted something that could take all the things floating around in my head and help me answer:

**“Okay. What should I actually do right now?”**

I couldn't find exactly what I wanted, so I made a prototype for it.
